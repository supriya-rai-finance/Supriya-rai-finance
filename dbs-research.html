Five-year research observation: DBS has materially expanded its earnings base over the five-year period. Total income increased from approximately SGD 14.2 billion in 2021 to SGD 22.9 billion in 2025, while profit before tax increased from approximately SGD 7.8 billion to SGD 13.1 billion.

Profitability also remained strong. ROE increased from 12.5% in 2021 to 16.2% in 2025, although it moderated from the 18.0% level recorded in 2023 and 2024.

NIM followed the interest-rate cycle, rising from 1.45% in 2021 to 2.15% in 2023 before moderating to 2.01% in 2025. This makes future margin trends an important variable for earnings analysis.

At the same time, asset quality remained relatively stable, with the NPL ratio improving from 1.3% in 2021 to 1.0% in 2025.

Analyst focus: The next stage of this research is to assess whether fee-income growth, wealth-management activity, balance-sheet growth and capital strength can offset potential pressure on net interest margins.
04 — Earnings Drivers

What drove FY2025 performance?

01

Net interest income & NIM

Net interest income remained resilient at approximately SGD 14.5 billion in FY2025, while NIM moderated to 2.01% from 2.13% in FY2024. The movement reflects the changing interest-rate environment and the importance of balance-sheet and deposit management.

02

Fee income & wealth management

Net fee and commission income increased to approximately SGD 4.9 billion in FY2025 from SGD 4.2 billion in FY2024. Wealth management was an important contributor, providing greater diversification beyond traditional interest income.

03

Wealth management momentum

DBS reported SGD 39 billion of net new money during 2025. Continued wealth-management activity is therefore an important area to monitor for recurring fee generation, client asset growth and earnings diversification.

05 — Banking Economics

Net interest margin & rates

Why NIM matters

Net interest margin measures the spread between interest earned on assets and interest paid on funding, relative to the interest-earning asset base.

For a bank, changes in benchmark interest rates can materially affect loan yields, deposit costs and therefore net interest income.

FY2025 observation

DBS operated in an environment of sharply lower benchmark interest rates in Singapore and Hong Kong.

The bank reported that proactive balance-sheet hedging and strong deposit inflows helped offset pressure on interest margins.

09 — Valuation Framework

How I would value a bank

Traditional FCFF-based DCF models are less suitable for banks because debt is part of the operating and funding structure. A bank-focused framework can instead combine earnings, book value, ROE, dividends and relative valuation.

Primary framework

  • Price-to-book relative to sustainable ROE
  • P/E based on forward earnings
  • Dividend yield and payout sustainability
  • Earnings sensitivity to NIM and credit costs

Supporting framework

  • Dividend Discount Model
  • Residual / excess-return framework
  • Peer comparison with Singapore banks
  • ROE versus cost-of-equity analysis

11 — Key Risks

Risk factors

Interest-rate risk

Further rate changes can pressure lending yields, deposit economics and NIM.

Credit risk

Deterioration in borrower quality or specific sector exposures could increase credit costs.

Market risk

Capital-market volatility can affect trading activity, wealth-management flows and client sentiment.

Regulatory & capital risk

Changes in capital requirements, regulation and tax rules can affect returns and distributions.
