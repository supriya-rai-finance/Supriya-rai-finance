05 — Banking Economics

Net interest margin & rates

Why NIM matters

Net interest margin measures the spread between interest earned on assets and interest paid on funding, relative to the interest-earning asset base.

For a bank, changes in benchmark interest rates can materially affect loan yields, deposit costs and therefore net interest income.

FY2025 observation

DBS operated in an environment of sharply lower benchmark interest rates in Singapore and Hong Kong.

The bank reported that proactive balance-sheet hedging and strong deposit inflows helped offset pressure on interest margins.

09 — Valuation Framework

How I would value a bank

Traditional FCFF-based DCF models are less suitable for banks because debt is part of the operating and funding structure. A bank-focused framework should therefore combine price-to-book, sustainable ROE, earnings, dividends and relative valuation.

Primary valuation framework

  • Price-to-book relative to sustainable ROE
  • P/E based on forward earnings
  • Dividend yield and payout sustainability
  • Earnings sensitivity to NIM and credit costs

Supporting framework

  • Dividend Discount Model
  • Residual / excess-return framework
  • Peer comparison with Singapore banks
  • ROE versus cost-of-equity analysis

Bank valuation logic

For a bank, book value and the return generated on that capital are closely linked to valuation. A higher sustainable ROE relative to the cost of equity can support a higher justified price-to-book multiple, while weaker profitability or higher perceived risk can place pressure on the multiple.

Core metric Price-to-book (P/B)
Profitability metric ROE
Income metric Forward EPS & dividend yield
Risk variables NIM, credit costs & capital
DBS valuation perspective: DBS generated a 16.2% ROE in FY2025 while maintaining a 17.0% CET1 ratio. This combination of profitability and capital strength provides an important starting point for assessing the valuation multiple investors are willing to assign to the bank.

The analysis should not rely on a single valuation metric. P/B should be considered alongside sustainable ROE, forward earnings, dividend capacity and the outlook for net interest margins and credit costs.

Analyst focus: The key question is whether DBS can sustain attractive returns on equity while continuing to grow fee income, wealth-management activity and the balance sheet without materially increasing credit or capital risk.

Next step in the model: A full valuation model would compare DBS with major Singapore banking peers and calculate an implied valuation using P/B, P/E and dividend-based approaches.
10 — Catalysts & Risks

Variables that could change the earnings outlook

01

Wealth-management growth

Continued net new money and client investment activity could support recurring fee income and diversify the earnings mix beyond traditional interest income.

02

Balance-sheet growth

Growth in customer loans and deposits could support future income generation if balance-sheet expansion remains consistent with capital and liquidity objectives.

03

Capital returns

Dividend growth and potential share repurchases can influence shareholder returns and per-share earnings, subject to capital requirements and management decisions.

04

Net interest margin

Changes in benchmark rates, deposit pricing and funding costs could affect NIM and therefore remain an important variable for future net interest income.

05

Credit quality

Higher credit losses or deterioration in asset quality could increase provisions and place pressure on earnings and return on equity.

06

Regulatory capital

Changes in capital requirements or risk-weighted assets could influence the amount of capital available for balance-sheet growth and shareholder distributions.

Research framework: The catalysts and risks above should be monitored alongside DBS's earnings, NIM, fee income, loan growth, deposit growth, asset quality and capital ratios.

Key monitoring question: Can DBS continue to grow fee-based businesses and its balance sheet while maintaining strong capitalisation, stable credit quality and attractive returns on equity?

This framework is intended to identify variables that could influence future financial performance. It does not represent a price target or investment recommendation.

13 — Research Conclusion

Key takeaways from the analysis

Earnings & profitability

DBS has expanded its earnings base materially over the five-year period. FY2025 total income reached approximately SGD 22.9 billion and profit before tax reached approximately SGD 13.1 billion, while ROE remained strong at 16.2%.

Balance sheet & capital

Customer loans and deposits increased during FY2025, while the CET1 ratio remained at 17.0%. The NPL ratio improved to 1.0%, providing an important indicator of continued asset-quality resilience.

Earnings diversification

Net fee and commission income increased to approximately SGD 4.9 billion in FY2025. Wealth-management activity and net new money are therefore important areas to monitor as DBS continues to diversify its earnings base.

Key variables to monitor

Future research should focus on NIM, loan and deposit growth, credit costs, wealth-management flows, capital returns and valuation relative to Singapore banking peers.

Research conclusion: The analysis indicates a banking franchise with strong profitability, substantial balance-sheet scale, diversified income streams and a solid capital position.

However, the future earnings profile remains sensitive to the interest-rate environment, net interest margins, credit quality, balance-sheet growth and the continued development of fee-generating businesses.

A complete investment assessment would therefore require current market valuation, peer comparison and scenario-based sensitivity analysis before reaching a valuation conclusion.
