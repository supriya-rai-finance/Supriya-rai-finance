Singapore Banking Peer Comparison

DBS Group vs OCBC vs UOB — a comparative analysis of financial performance, profitability, balance-sheet strength, capital, valuation and key earnings drivers.

01 — Sector Overview

Three major Singapore banking franchises

DBS Group, OCBC and UOB are the three major Singapore banking groups. Their businesses span consumer banking, institutional banking, wealth management, treasury and markets, with different geographic and business exposures across Singapore and Southeast Asia.

DBS Group
SGX: D05

Regional banking franchise with consumer, institutional and wealth-management businesses.

OCBC
SGX: O39

Diversified banking group with significant Singapore, Malaysia and regional operations, alongside insurance and wealth-management businesses.

UOB
SGX: U11

ASEAN-focused banking group spanning consumer, commercial and institutional banking.

Research objective: Compare the three franchises using common banking metrics rather than relying on a single performance measure.
02 — Business Model

Different franchises, overlapping banking engines

DBS

The franchise combines consumer banking, institutional banking, wealth management and treasury activities. Wealth-management activity and deposit growth were important contributors to FY2025 performance.

OCBC

OCBC combines banking with insurance and wealth management. In FY2025, wealth-management income reached a record SGD 5.60 billion and represented 38% of total income.

UOB

UOB maintains a strong ASEAN orientation with consumer, commercial and wholesale banking operations. FY2025 performance benefited from fee momentum across its diversified franchise.

Analyst lens

Business mix matters because interest income, fee income, wealth management, insurance and regional expansion can respond differently to changes in rates and economic conditions.

08 — Valuation Framework

How I would compare bank valuations

Price-to-book

P/B is particularly relevant for banks because book value represents an important measure of the capital base supporting lending and other financial activities.

ROE relationship

P/B should be considered alongside sustainable ROE and the cost of equity. A valuation multiple should therefore be interpreted together with expected profitability.

P/E

Forward P/E can provide a second earnings-based lens, particularly when comparing expected earnings growth and profitability across peers.

Dividend yield

Dividend yield and payout sustainability are relevant because capital returns form an important part of the shareholder-return profile of mature banks.

Analyst framework: I would avoid using a single valuation metric. P/B, P/E, dividend yield, sustainable ROE, earnings growth, capital requirements and credit risk should be considered together.

11 — Research Conclusion

Key takeaways

Scale

DBS, OCBC and UOB each operate large regional banking franchises, but their balance-sheet scale and geographic mix differ materially.

Profitability

FY2025 ROE was 16.2% for DBS, 12.6% for OCBC and 9.6% for UOB. ROE should be assessed alongside capital, risk and earnings sustainability.

Risk profile

NPL ratios remained relatively contained, although reported levels differed across the three banks. Credit costs and provision coverage remain important quarterly monitoring variables.

Valuation

A complete peer valuation would require current market prices, forward earnings, book values, expected ROE and dividend assumptions rather than relying only on historical financial ratios.

Analyst conclusion: The three banks share a common Singapore banking foundation, but their earnings mix, regional exposure, profitability, capital position and credit profile create meaningful differences for comparative research.

The next stage of this analysis would be to connect these operating differences to current market valuation and build a scenario-based peer valuation model.
